SECTION 01

Welcome

Start here

DynastyOS is a companion app for EA Sports College Football 27. It reads your dynasty save and turns it into a clean, browsable archive — your roster, schedule, stats, standings, recruiting, awards, and more — that you can explore any time, without the game running.

When to sync — the whole cheat sheet

If you read nothing else, read this. A sync is how the app captures your dynasty. Starting a brand-new dynasty, here's when to do it, walking through a season in order:

  • Week 0 — your very first sync, and the first sync of every season after. Before you play a single game, the game names its Preseason All-America teams; syncing now captures them and sets which school your coach belongs to for the year. On a fresh dynasty, this is where your archive begins.
  • During the season — any time you like. Totally safe: every sync just refreshes that season in place, and nothing is ever duplicated. Sync on an awards week if you want each honor recorded the moment the game hands it out.
  • The End of Season Recap — lock in the season. After your bowl or playoff and the awards ceremony. This captures your final record, postseason result, and every award, and it locks the season so nothing can overwrite it later.
  • The "Players Leaving" step — capture who's moving on. Just after the recap, the offseason reaches a step where the game decides who declares for the NFL, who graduates, and who's transferring out (and why). Sync here to record all of that on your Transfers & Departures page — it's the one moment that data exists, and the app locks the roster shortly after, so don't advance past it without syncing.

The good news: you can't break your archive by syncing at the "wrong" time anymore. Once a season is finished, the app automatically protects it — later syncs can't overwrite it (the recap and Players-Leaving syncs both still land, they just add to the same locked season) — and the coaching carousel is handled for you (Section 05). Sync at Week 0, at the recap, and at Players Leaving, and you're set.

The single most important idea

The app reads your save. It does not change your game. Importing, syncing, and browsing never touch your dynasty — they just take a snapshot and show it to you beautifully.

There is exactly one exception, and it only happens when you deliberately ask for it: the editing tools (Section 10) and the experimental tools (Section 12) can write back to your save. Everywhere else, you can click around with zero risk.

In plain terms

Think of it like a scrapbook that fills itself in from your game. Looking through the scrapbook never changes the game. Only the clearly-marked "edit" buttons do — and even those back up your save first.

Why you'd want it

A real history

The game only remembers this season. DynastyOS keeps every season you sync, so year 12 can still look back at year 1.

Everything in one place

Roster, schedule, stats, recruiting, awards, standings, and the whole nation's players — all a click apart.

Your story, shareable

Tag screenshots to games and players, and export a self-contained web page of your dynasty's history.

Made to look good

Your team's colors run through the whole app, so it always feels like your program.

SECTION 02

Getting Started

Setup

Three short steps and you're in: install the app, import your save, then get into the habit of syncing. Here's each one.

Step 1 — Install (two parts, the first time only)

DynastyOS comes as two downloads:

  • The app (DynastyOS Setup) — the program itself. It's small, and it's the only part that changes when you update.
  • The image data (DynastyOS Image Data) — about 1 GB of player faces, team logos, and trophies. You install this once.

The first time, run both. When the image installer asks where to put the data, pick any folder you like — the app finds it automatically afterward. From then on, every update is just the small app installer, and your image data stays exactly where it is.

Because the app isn't signed by a big publisher, Windows may show a blue SmartScreen warning the first time. That's normal for small apps — click More info, then Run anyway.

If it says “image data not found”

The app just needs to know where your image folder is — click Locate image data folder and browse to it. This can happen if you moved the folder or set up on a new PC. You can also see and change the location any time under Preferences → Image data folder.

Step 2 — Import your dynasty

  1. Open DynastyOS. You'll land on the Dashboard.
  2. Click Import Dynasty and pick your save file. EA's saves usually have no file extension you'll recognize, so the picker shows everything — just choose your dynasty file.
  3. The app reads it and builds your first season. Done.

Your save file is never modified by importing. The app only makes its own private copy of the information it needs.

Step 3 — The one habit that matters: sync every season

A sync captures your save exactly as it looks right now. Once you advance to the next year in-game, this season's detail is gone from the save forever — the game only ever keeps the current state.

The golden rule

For a normal season, sync at the End of Season Recap — after your bowl or playoff and the awards ceremony. That's the moment the season's story is complete: final record, postseason result, and every award locked in. Then sync once more at the offseason's Players Leaving step to record who's departing (NFL, graduations, transfers out) — see below.

Syncing more than once in a season is safe; it just refreshes that year's data in place, and nothing is ever duplicated. It's one click — no file picker — because the app already knows where your save lives.

And you can't sync at the "wrong" time. The moment a season finishes, the app locks it — so if you sync again later (deep into the offseason, mid-carousel, National Signing Day) that finished season stays exactly as you left it. You get the normal "Synced" confirmation and nothing good is overwritten. Sync freely.

Moments worth an extra sync

  • Week 0 of a new season — the game names its Preseason All-America watch-list teams before any games are played. Sync at Week 0 to capture them (see Section 07).
  • The awards week — if you want each honor recorded exactly when the game hands it out, sync that week. Weekly honors update on every in-season sync.
  • The offseason's "Players Leaving" step — just after the recap, this is when the game decides who declares for the NFL, who graduates, and who's transferring out (and why). Sync here to fill in your Transfers & Departures page; it's the one moment that data exists before the roster churns for next year.

If you miss a season

The next time you sync, the app recovers that year's league-wide facts — the national champion, each conference champion, and the major awards — as a “History Only” season. Your own team's roster and game-by-game stats for a skipped year can't come back, because the save simply doesn't keep them. Recovering the trophies is a nice consolation, not a substitute for syncing.

Best possible archive

Start the app on a brand-new dynasty and sync from season one. If you first import a save that's already several years deep, only the current season arrives in full detail.

SECTION 03

How It's Organized

The map

The app is split into five hubs, grouped by whose story they tell. Once you know the five, you always know where to look.

Coach Hub

The person. Your coach's career, record, staff, and season-by-season timeline — even across different schools.

Team Hub

Your program. Roster, schedule, statistics & trends, transfers & rivalries, honors, and history for the selected season.

NCAA Hub

The nation. National rankings, every team's players, standings, national awards, All-America teams, and the record book.

Recruit Hub

The future. Your recruiting board and the entire national pool of prospects, with the tools to work them.

Media Hub

Your memories. A per-season gallery of screenshots and clips — tag them to games and players, and they link back everywhere.

Getting around

The top tabs switch between sections — Coach, Program, NCAA, Recruiting, Media. Inside each, a row of smaller tabs picks the page, and paired pages (Roster/Transfers, Schedule/Rivalries, Statistics/Analytics, Season Awards/Weekly Honors) share one tab with a switch above them. The left sidebar lists your dynasties — switch programs with one click. Preferences, the User Manual and About sit as icons in the top-right corner of the window, beside the minimise button.

The menus stay put. Section tabs, team tabs and the season switcher are pinned to the top of the window, so a long page — a full roster, a national player list — no longer means scrolling back to the top just to change year or tab. They're always where you left them.

Browsing another team

On any team page a team dropdown sits at the right of the sub-tabs — pick any program in the country and the page redraws for them. Faster still: hold Shift and tap ← or → to step through the list one team at a time. It loops, so holding Shift and tapping → from the last team brings you back to your own.

The season picker

Once a dynasty has more than one tracked season, a Season dropdown appears next to the tabs. Pick any year to see the app exactly as it stood then. Two kinds of season can appear:

  • A normal season — synced while it was live. Full detail: roster, schedule, standings, stats, awards, staff, everything.
  • A History Only season — recovered after the fact. You'll see that year's champions and major awards on the History tab, but not your own roster or game logs (see Section 02).
Remember it this way

Coach Hub answers “who is this coach?” Team Hub answers “how is this season going?” NCAA Hub answers “what's happening around the country?” Recruit Hub answers “who's coming next?” Media Hub answers “what did it look like?”

SECTION 04

The Dashboard

Home base

The Dashboard is the first thing you see. Every dynasty you've imported shows up here as a card, and each card carries a small row of buttons.

What each button does

  • Sync — re-reads the exact save on record, no file picker. Your everyday button; use it before advancing each season.
  • Export — saves a self-contained web page of your dynasty's history (record book, résumé, timeline, milestones) that opens in any browser, no app required. Great for sharing.
  • Backup — makes a timestamped copy of your actual game save, stored safely outside the game folder. Worth doing before any editing session.
  • Delete — removes the dynasty from the app only. It never touches your game save.

Import & Relink

The big Import Dynasty button adds a save. If you rename, move, or restore your save file, the app matches dynasties by their exact file location — so a moved file can look brand-new. When it notices, the app offers to link the file to the existing dynasty instead of making a duplicate. Just confirm the prompt.

Good to know

Import, Sync, Export, Backup, and Delete are all safe for your game. The only tools that ever write into your save are the editing and experimental features, covered in Sections 10 and 12.

SECTION 05

Coach Hub

The person

Coach Hub is your coach's career page. It's the story of you — not just this team, but everywhere you've been.

What you'll find

  • Profile & lifetime record — tracked by the game across your entire career, not just what you've imported here.
  • Season-high rankings — the best your team reached in the Media, Coaches, and Playoff polls this season.
  • Your staff — the coordinators and assistants around you.
  • Career timeline — every season you've coached, in order, even across different schools if you've moved on.

Click the pencil on any coach card to open the editor (see Section 10).

Moving to a new school

When you take a job at a new program, DynastyOS keeps your career as one continuous journey — past seasons stay tied to the school where you actually earned them (right down to that school's logo, colors, and record), while the new school begins its own chapter.

You don't have to do anything special for this to work. The app used to ask you to sync in a careful order around the coaching carousel — that's no longer necessary. DynastyOS now knows exactly where your save is in the calendar, so it handles the timing for you:

  • The season you just finished is locked to your old school the moment it ends — with its final record and awards — so nothing that happens in the carousel can rewrite it.
  • Your new school becomes official at Week 0 of the next season. Sync then (you'd be syncing anyway for preseason honors) and your new chapter opens cleanly under the right team.
In plain terms

Change jobs whenever the game lets you, and sync whenever you like — including right in the middle of the carousel. Your finished season stays put at your old school, and your new school picks up at Week 0. No special order, no danger window to avoid.

SECTION 06

Team Hub

Your program

Team Hub is your program for the selected season. A persistent masthead shows the logo, name, and record; the tabs below take you everywhere. To keep the row tidy, a few closely-related pages share one tab and split with a small toggle on top.

▸ Overview▸ Roster▸ Schedule▸ Statistics▸ Honors▸ History

Overview

The season at a glance: record, conference record, poll ranks, recruiting-class rank, prestige, roster size, and average overall — plus recent and upcoming games, top players, and any trophies won.

Roster — with a Roster | Transfers toggle

Every player on your team. Search by name, filter by unit, position, or class — redshirts show as RS Fr., RS So. and so on, and each redshirt year is its own choice in the class filter — and sort by any column. Switch between a compact List and a visual Gallery. Click any player to open their full profile (below); on the current season, a pencil lets you edit them. Flip to Transfers for who came and who left, season over season: incoming and outgoing moves (each tagged with the reason), plus a “Left the program” list of players who went to the NFL (with projected round) or graduated. Departure details fill in once you sync during the offseason's “Players Leaving” step.

Schedule — with a Schedule | Rivalries toggle

Your full slate — opponents, ranks, home/away, results, and running record. Conference games, rivalries, and bowls are marked, and each row opens the game's box score. Flip to Rivalries for your all-time head-to-head series record against every opponent you've faced across synced seasons — series wins, streaks, and average margin.

Statistics — with a Season | Trends toggle

Team and player leaders across passing, rushing, receiving, defense, kicking, punting, and returns — with leader cards that put a face and number to each category. Flip to Trends for charts that track how your program moves across every season you've synced — the payoff of syncing each year.

Honors — Season Awards | Weekly Honors

All the hardware your players earned, together: Season Awards and Weekly Honors under one tab, with a toggle between them.

History

Every school's real history, not just yours. Three parts, and the page is clear about where each comes from:

  • All-time — the program's whole record: overall and home, bowls and New Year's Six, conference and national titles, playoff appearances, weeks ranked, rivalry record, Heismans, All-Americans, players drafted, and top recruiting classes. Real history, added to as your dynasty runs.
  • Record book — the school's career and single-season records with the holder's name, position and year, on a Career | Season switch. These are real records, and your players can take them over: put up a big enough year and the name in that box becomes one of yours.
  • Season by season — year, coach, record, conference record and finish, final ranking, and how the postseason ended. This one starts when your dynasty starts, because that's the point the save begins itemising seasons.

Use the team dropdown to read any program in the country this way. (Your season photos & clips live in the Media Hub — Section 09.)

The player profile

Click any player, anywhere in the app, and you get a workspace built around five questions rather than a wall of tabs. Move between them with the menu at the top; ← and → step to the previous or next player without closing anything.

▸ Overview▸ Performance▸ Ratings▸ Journey▸ Showcase

Overview — who is this player

The short answer, in one screen: his profile and his development on top, a snapshot of the season and his Player DNA beneath, and his latest career event across the foot. The header owns his identity — number, team, position, class, name, overall — so nothing is repeated twice.

Performance — how is he playing

A This Season | Career | Games switch, ordered by how quickly each part answers: the headline numbers, then his recent form, then the full statistical line, then the game-by-game log. Any row in the game log opens that game's box score. Recent form and Best Game appear only once there's more than one game to compare — one game isn't a trend, and it isn't a “best” either.

Ratings — how good is he

Opens with Player DNA: his biggest strength, his weakest link, and what to develop next — drawn from the ratings that actually matter at his position, not a generic average. Below that are the rating groups relevant to that position, with View all ratings holding the complete list.

Journey — what has happened to him

One chronological career story instead of four separate lists. Milestones, national awards, All-America selections, weekly honors, team awards and your own notes all interleave into a single timeline, filtered by All · Milestones · Honors · Notes.

Showcase — what you've made of him

Everything you authored about this player, on a Cards | Media switch: the trading cards you built, and the photos you tagged him in.

Trading cards & the card book

Showcase opens on that player's cards, three to a row. Click one to open it full size and use ← → to walk the set; click + to start another. A player can have as many as you like, and the small dot under a card marks the one that pops up when you hover his name anywhere in the app.

Everything for a card lives with that card, in the expanded view — so there's no wrong card to hit:

  • The pencil opens its editor. Upload Photo takes a screenshot from your computer; Media Photo reuses anything already tagged to him in the Media Hub. Drag the photo to frame it and use Zoom to fill the card.
  • Show on card turns the overall, the name, the profile line, the stat row and the team logo on or off — so a clean photo-and-name card is one click away. The card keeps that look everywhere: the grid, the hover preview and the exported PNG.
  • One game, or the whole year. The stat line can come from the season or from a single game — pick it from the dropdown and the card celebrates that Saturday instead. Choose up to four stats to run across the bottom.
  • The star adds the card to your card book (the book icon in the top bar), which collects every starred card in the dynasty, a page per season. Export saves a card as a PNG.
A card is a printed moment

The year, the school and the profile line lock in the moment you make a card. Nothing later — a transfer, a class change, a re-sync — quietly reprints an old card as this season. That's also why cards don't change when you switch years, and why a card's stat line can only be re-picked from the season it was made in.

Peek without clicking

Rest your cursor on a player's name and their card pops up as a quick preview. You can turn this on or off — and set how long to wait — under Preferences → Player card preview.

Program editor — for imported teams

A Teambuilder school imports over a real school's slot: your team keeps that slot, but its name, colours and art token are all new. The app ships artwork for the real schools, not for one it has never seen — so an imported program shows a placeholder logo, a generic helmet, and no uniform or coach polo.

Program editor, on your Team Hub just above Program budget, is where you fix that. Identity sets your stadium name and city, which then show on your schedule and on every game's info page. Artwork takes four uploads:

  • Program logo — 1024 × 1024, transparent
  • Helmet — 1024 × 1024, transparent, facing left (the other side is mirrored for you)
  • Uniform — 512 × 512, sits over a player portrait
  • Coach polo — 512 × 512, sits over a coach portrait

Four uploads dress a team completely, and the art then appears everywhere team art appears — mastheads, matchup graphics, roster portraits, coach cards and trading cards. The uniform and polo are the fussy ones: they lay directly on top of a portrait, so the collar and shoulders have to line up or every player will look slightly off. Upload, check a few players, adjust.

Nothing is written to your save

Your files are copied into the app's own storage, so moving or deleting the originals won't break anything. Remove an upload and the built-in artwork comes back. Edits belong to that dynasty only — an imported team that happens to share a name with a real school never changes that school in your other saves.

SECTION 07

NCAA Hub

The nation

The NCAA Hub zooms out from your team to the whole country. Same clean look, national scope.

▸ Overview▸ Scores▸ Players▸ Standings▸ Annual Awards▸ All-America & All-Conf▸ Record Book

Overview

A national snapshot — the top of the polls and the shape of the season around the country.

Scores — every game in the country

The full national scoreboard, week by week, with two dropdowns: the week, and which games — All NCAA, Top 25, Upsets, or any single conference. Each option carries a live count, so you can see what's there before you pick it.

Every box carries the game's own mark: the bowl's logo, the playoff round, the conference championship, the rivalry shield, or the conference. Ranks sit before the team name. Click any game — yours or two teams you've never played — for the full Game Information box score.

What counts as an upset

An unranked team beating a ranked one. It's measured against the poll as it stood at your last sync — a save only ever keeps the current poll, not a week-by-week history — so late in a season it reads against the final one.

Players — every player in the nation

This is the national twin of your team Roster: every player in the country in one searchable place — thousands of them. It works exactly like your own roster, with the same search, filters, sorting, and List/Gallery views, plus two extras for the national scope: filter by conference or team, and a Team column with logos. Because the field is enormous, it shows a batch at a time (sorted by overall by default) and asks you to narrow with filters or search to see more. Click anyone to open their full profile.

Standings

Every conference's standings, including divisions where they exist, with conference champions marked once a season is decided.

Annual Awards & All-America

The national, single-winner awards (Heisman and the rest) and the All-America and All-Conference teams. Winners from your program are highlighted so they're easy to spot.

The All-America & All-Conf page has a Postseason / Preseason switch — the earned end-of-year teams, or the game's preseason watch list. Postseason honors lock in at the End of Season Recap; to capture a year's preseason teams, sync at Week 0 of that season.

Record Book

The league's built-in career and single-season records.

SECTION 08

Recruit Hub

The future

The Recruit Hub is where next year's team takes shape. Two pages: your own board, and the entire national pool.

My Board

Every prospect you've added to your board in-game — filter and sort them, then open one for their school interest, an athletic snapshot, and the editing tools.

National Recruits

The whole country's recruiting pool for the season — around three thousand prospects. Filter by position, stars, class, home state, or commitment stage, and open any prospect's full profile.

Hidden ratings, by design

To keep recruiting true to the spirit of the game, a prospect's overall and athletic ratings start hidden — you scout on rank, stars, and film. You can reveal one prospect at a time from their profile, or flip on Reveal all recruit ratings in Preferences to see everything at once. Your choice.

Experimental · Force Commit

An optional, experimental tool can force a boarded recruit to commit to your team so they actually join next season. It writes to your save, so it's off until you enable Experimental save editing in Preferences — and it always backs up and verifies first. Full safety details are in Section 12.

SECTION 09

Media Hub

Your memories

Media Hub is its own top-level section (right of Recruit Hub) — a per-season gallery for your dynasty's screenshots and clips that does clever things once you tag them.

Adding photos

  1. Click Add photos / videos and pick your files — or just drag them from your computer and drop them onto the grid. Either way they're copied into the app's own library, so the gallery keeps working even if you move or delete the originals. A progress bar tracks the import.
  2. Click a thumbnail to open the viewer (it always opens centered), then Edit details to pick which game it's from, tag the players in it, and write a description.
  3. Move through the viewer with the on-screen arrows or your keyboard's ← → keys.

Zoom, pan & framing

An open photo zooms. Drag the slider at the bottom-left, or scroll the wheel over the picture, to go in up to 5× — then drag the photo itself to move around it. Double-click snaps back to fit.

Found a crop you like? Save framing keeps it: the photo opens that way every time, and its thumbnail shows the same crop. Reset gives the whole picture back.

Your file is never touched

Framing is only a note about how to show the photo — the full original is always there underneath. That's also why a trading card is unaffected: putting a media photo on a card takes its own copy with its own zoom, so the same shot can be cropped one way in your gallery and framed completely differently on the card. Changing either one never touches the other.

Organizing & batch edits

  • Reorder by dragging. Uploaded out of order? Drag any photo to a new spot and the order sticks.
  • Batch edits. Click Select, tick the photos you want (or Select all), then set the game for all of them at once — perfect for a batch of shots from the same game — or delete them all together. No going in one by one.
  • Search by number. When tagging, if you don't know a name, start your search with # (for example #17) and the roster filters to whoever wears that number.
  • Delete from the grid. Each thumbnail has a small trash button so you can remove a shot without opening it. Deleting a photo (or a whole dynasty) removes the actual files from your computer — nothing is left lying around.
Tags do double duty

Anything you tag automatically appears under Showcase → Media on each tagged player's profile (across every season they appear in) and in a Media section on the linked game's page — no extra work. A tagged photo can also be reused as that player's trading card image (Section 06). Uploads land in whichever season the Season dropdown is set to, so switch seasons first if you're backfilling older memories. Supported files: PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF images and MP4 / WebM video.

SECTION 10

Editing Players & Coaches

Write access

This is the one place the app is not read-only. The pencil icon on any player or coach card opens an editor that genuinely writes back to your save.

What you can edit

Ratings, physical and mental attributes, skill caps, and portraits for players; profile details for coaches. Recruits have their own editable fields too. Changes take effect the next time you load that save in the game.

Overall, worked out for you

Change a rating and the Ratings tab shows what that player's ratings now come to, using the game's own formula for his position and archetype — so dropping a receiver's speed doesn't leave an overall that no longer describes him. The calculated number sits beside the saved one; press Set overall when you want it written.

Kickers, punters and free safeties keep a manual overall and say so. Their formulas changed in College Football 27 and the app won't show a number it can't stand behind.

Only on the current season

Editing is offered only on the live, current season. A past season is a frozen snapshot — there's nothing real left to write a change back to.

Before you edit

Run Backup first (Dashboard) so you always have a clean copy to return to. It only takes a second and it's the difference between “oops” and “disaster.”

Portraits

Both player and coach portraits can be changed here and carry into the game — pick a new one in the editor and it shows up the next time you load the save.

SECTION 11

Preferences & Tools

Make it yours

At the bottom-left, under your dynasty list, the Tools group holds Preferences, Stadiums, and Quick Help. Preferences opens with every section folded — tap a section header to expand it.

Preferences

  • Appearance — switch between Light and Dark. (There's no separate toggle cluttering the sidebar; it lives here.) The dark theme is a true black base, with your team color used only for accents.
  • Theme Source — Team colors follow your active dynasty; Default keeps a steady neutral palette.
  • Player card preview — resting on a player's name pops up their Trading Card. On by default; turn it off, or use the slider to set how long to hover before it appears.
  • Reveal all recruit ratings — show every prospect's hidden ratings at once, or leave them hidden and reveal one at a time.
  • Check for updates on startup — when on, the app quietly checks for a newer version each time it launches and lets you know if one's out. Turn it off to launch straight in; you can always check by hand from the About panel.
  • Experimental save editing — unlocks tools that write to your save (currently Force Commit). Off by default; see Section 12.

Stadiums

A small database of stadium names and locations used around the app. If one is wrong or missing, correct it here — your edits are saved on your computer and apply across every dynasty. This never changes your save file.

Keyboard shortcuts

The keyboard icon in the top-right corner opens the shortcut editor: every page in the app, and not one of them bound. Which page deserves a key depends on how you play, so the keys are yours to spend.

Click a shortcut field and press the combination you want. It needs Ctrl, Alt or Cmd (or a function key) — a bare letter would fire while you were reading a page. Combinations the app already uses are refused by name, one combination only ever means one page, and nothing fires while you're typing.

Quick Help

A built-in, plain-language how-to guide that grows over time — including a “When to sync” reference so you never miss the moments that matter. If something in the app isn't obvious, check Quick Help first. (This manual is the long version; Quick Help is the fast one, right inside the app.)

SECTION 12

Experimental Features

Handle with care

Some tools reach further than reading — they write into your dynasty save. They're powerful, genuinely useful, and clearly separated so you're never surprised by them.

How they stay safe

  • Opt-in. They're hidden until you turn on Experimental save editing in Preferences.
  • Backed up. Every write makes a fresh backup of your save first.
  • Verified. After writing, the app reopens the save and checks the change actually took. If it didn't, it restores your backup automatically.

Force Commit

Force Commit takes a recruit already on your board and locks them to your team, so they truly join your roster the following season. It marks them signed to your school and adds them to your incoming class — the list the game actually enrolls from.

The one rule that matters

Run experimental tools with College Football 27 fully closed, then reload the save in-game afterward. If the game is running while the app writes, its autosave can overwrite the change or show stale information. Close the game, force the commit, then open the game and load the save.

Experimental means exactly that: it works, and it's built defensively, but it's newer than the rest of the app. The backups are your safety net — keep one before a session, and you can always step back.

Tips & Best Practices

Pro moves

A short list that will make your archive better and your life easier.

  • Sync at the End of Season Recap. It's the whole game — one click before you advance, every year, to lock in the final record and awards.
  • Sync at Week 0 for preseason honors. That's when the game names its Preseason All-America teams.
  • Sync at "Players Leaving" for departures. Just after the recap — this is the only moment the game reveals who's off to the NFL, who graduated, and who transferred out. Miss it and your Transfers & Departures page stays blank for that year.
  • Changing schools? Just sync normally. The app handles the coaching carousel for you now — your finished season stays with your old school, your new school opens at Week 0. No special order (Section 05).
  • Start fresh for the fullest history. A new dynasty synced from year one gives you complete detail for every season.
  • Back up before editing. Two seconds now saves you a headache later.
  • Close the game before experimental writes. Then reload the save afterward.
  • Tag your media as you go. Tagged shots flow automatically onto player and game pages — a little effort now, a rich archive later.
  • Use the season dropdown. It's a time machine for your whole dynasty.
  • Export and share. The history export is a single web page anyone can open — no app needed.
If you only remember one thing

Sync every season before you advance. Everything good about this app is built on that one habit.

Honest Limitations

Straight talk

A good tool tells you what it can't do. Here's the honest list — most of these exist because the information simply isn't in the save file.

  • Full-league box scores need a fresh sync. The Game Information box score now works for every game in the country — both teams' player stats for your games and any other matchup (see NCAA Hub → Scores). This lights up for seasons synced with this version onward; seasons archived earlier keep just your own games' detail, because the league-wide box scores weren't captured at the time.
  • Poll history. Rankings reflect the current week at the moment you sync — there's no week-by-week trend from before the app started tracking your dynasty.
  • History-Only seasons stay that way. A skipped season can't become a full one after the fact; the underlying roster and stats no longer exist in the save.
  • The development curve builds one season at a time. A save file only ever carries the current season's roster, so a single import gives you one full season of a player's arc. Sync each year and the curve fills in behind you — there's no way to recover the years before you started.
  • Ratings come from the live save. That means they're only available for the current season's roster. Open a past-season profile and it says so plainly rather than guessing at numbers that were never archived.
  • Program editor covers your own program. Other imported teams in the same save still fall back to placeholder artwork for now.

These are the honest edges of what the save file makes possible today.

Always improving

We're constantly looking for ways around these limits, and we're open to findings from the community. If you've discovered something the save makes possible that we haven't tapped yet, we'd genuinely love to hear it.
